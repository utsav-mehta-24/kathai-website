**KATHAI**

**Consumer Journey Analysis**

**Premium Hot Chocolate Audience**

Bengaluru launch market | India expansion market

**Scope of this document**

A focused, pre-launch analysis of Awareness, Consideration, Purchase, Retention and Advocacy. It is designed as a decision tool, not a generic marketing funnel.

Prepared October 2026

**HOW TO READ THIS JOURNEY**

# Audience frame and analytical method

Harvard Business School Online defines a customer journey map as a visual representation of customer interactions and recommends grounding stages, touchpoints, emotions, motivations and pain points in research. It also warns against a single generic map and against treating the journey as static. \[1\]

**Pre-launch interpretation**

Because Kathai has not launched, this map is a research-led hypothesis. It should guide proposition, sampling and channel experiments, then be updated using interviews, tasting feedback, analytics, reviews and repeat-purchase data.

## Evidence informing the hypothesis

**Audience weighting: The journey remains inclusive of premium adults across life stages, but Gen Z receives additional emphasis in discovery, creative expression, personalization, social proof, cultural collaborations and advocacy. This reflects research describing Indian Gen Z as experience collectors who value authenticity, self-expression, wellbeing and stories behind beverages. \[13\]\[14\]\[15\]**

- India research indicates taste, price and nutrition are major food choice factors, while transparency and trust matter increasingly. \[2\]\[3\]
- Bengaluru consumers are described as seeking quality, variety, convenience and experience across specialty coffee, premium chocolate and adjacent everyday categories. \[4\]
- Coffee and café visibility has trained urban consumers to view a beverage as a lifestyle experience, creating a useful reference point for premium hot chocolate. \[5\]\[6\]

**FIVE-STAGE ARCHITECTURE**

# Full journey at a glance

The journey is non-linear. A customer may discover Kathai through social content, validate it through trusted reviews, experience a ₹450–₹600 signature drink at a brand-led space or curated partner venue, return for a new story or ritual, and later advocate through hosting or gifting. Digital channels should create anticipation and access, while the physical experience delivers the value. \[7\]\[13\]\[14\]

| **Lens**          | **Customer reality**                       | **Motivation**           | **Barrier**                                 | **Touchpoints**                                             | **Kathai response**                                                          |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Awareness**     | "Is premium hot chocolate relevant to me?" | Curiosity; mood fit      | Childish or seasonal image                  | Creators, cafés, gifting, search                            | Make the category adult, sensory and culturally relevant                     |
| **Consideration** | "Why Kathai, and why at this price?"       | Quality; trust; identity | Taste uncertainty; sugar and value concerns | Product page, reviews, sampling                             | Prove taste, ingredients, preparation and occasion                           |
| **Purchase**      | "Can I try this with low risk?"            | Immediate reward         | Availability; delivery; commitment          | Flagship space, pop-up, curated café partner, premium event | Guided tasting, signature service ritual and seamless reservation or payment |
| **Retention**     | "Does it deserve a place in my routine?"   | Comfort; consistency     | Preparation friction; novelty fade          | Pack, CRM, returnment                                       | Build habit, variety and reliable quality                                    |
| **Advocacy**      | "Is this worth recommending or gifting?"   | Pride; generosity        | No share-worthy story                       | UGC, referral, gifting, hosting                             | Create social currency and beautiful gifting                                 |

## The central strategic tension

**Kathai must create a category and a habit at the same time**

Awareness requires education because premium drinking chocolate is less established than coffee. Retention requires simplicity because a beautiful first cup will not become a business unless it becomes an easy, repeatable ritual.

## Recommended north-star behavior

Move customers from "I tried an interesting hot chocolate" to "This is my Kathai moment." That shift turns product trial into a named personal ritual and gives the brand a platform for retention and advocacy.

**STAGE 1 | BECOME RELEVANT BEFORE BECOMING PREFERRED**

# Awareness

At awareness, Kathai is not only competing for brand recognition. It must first establish premium hot chocolate as a credible adult beverage and meaningful occasion. Café culture has expanded because urban consumers increasingly seek premium beverages and environments for work, leisure and socialization. \[5\]\[6\]

| **Lens**       | **Customer reality**                                          | **Motivation**         | **Barrier**                                          | **Touchpoints**                             | **Kathai response**                                            |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mindset**    | "I know coffee and desserts; hot chocolate feels occasional." | Novelty and comfort    | Low category salience                                | Instagram, cafés, short video               | Show adult occasions: evening pause, monsoon, reading, hosting |
| **Emotion**    | Curious but not yet committed                                 | Sensory desire         | Fear of excessive sweetness                          | Visual content, aroma cues, creator tasting | Use texture, steam, cocoa origin and restrained luxury         |
| **Proof need** | A reason to notice and remember                               | Distinctiveness        | Another generic chocolate mix                        | Packaging, founder story, PR                | Own "story in a cup" with a clear visual code                  |
| **Bengaluru**  | Open to specialty formats and everyday premiumisation         | Experimentation        | Crowded café and direct brand relationship discovery | Café collaborations, office districts       | Seed hyperlocally in high-fit communities                      |
| **India**      | Premium interest but uneven category familiarity              | Aspiration and gifting | Price and relevance vary by city                     | Creators, marketplaces, festive discovery   | Translate the ritual across languages and occasions            |

## Recommended awareness system

- Category education: explain drinking chocolate versus cocoa powder, expected texture, cocoa intensity and preparation.
- Occasion building: link Kathai to monsoon evenings, post-work decompression, reading, conversation, hosting and thoughtful gifting.
- Memory asset: repeat a consistent cup silhouette, cocoa palette, sensory language and the "Kathai moment" idea.
- Credible discovery: prioritize food creators, baristas, pastry chefs, design communities and premium lifestyle micro-creators over broad celebrity reach.

**Awareness success is not reach alone**

Track qualified discovery: saves, profile visits, branded search, tasting sign-ups, experience waitlist, café trials and percentage of viewers who correctly understand what Kathai is.

**STAGE 2 | CONVERT CURIOSITY INTO JUSTIFIED DESIRE**

# Consideration

Food consideration is sensory and trust-heavy. Consumers cannot taste a direct brand relationship beverage before purchase, so they use ingredient transparency, reviews, visual cues, provenance, preparation effort and price as proxies. Taste, price and nutritional value remain central food-choice factors in India. \[2\]\[3\]\[8\]

| **Lens**          | **Customer reality**               | **Motivation**          | **Barrier**                     | **Touchpoints**                           | **Kathai response**                                         |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Core question** | "Will I genuinely enjoy it?"       | Taste confidence        | Sensory uncertainty             | Sampling, reviews, videos                 | Offer tasting notes and a low-risk discovery experience     |
| **Premium proof** | "What makes it worth more?"        | Craft and provenance    | Premium as experienceaging-only | Ingredient page, origin story             | Show cocoa content, sourcing, formulation and serving yield |
| **Adult fit**     | "Is it too sweet or childish?"     | Sophistication          | Sugar concern                   | Nutrition panel, variants                 | Offer intensity scale and balanced sweetness                |
| **Usability**     | "Can I make café quality at home?" | Control and convenience | Lumps, equipment, time          | How-to content, menu and service guidance | Simple recipe plus elevated ritual option                   |
| **Social proof**  | "Do people like me value it?"      | Belonging               | Few reviews at launch           | Creator trials, beta panel                | Publish specific, credible tasting feedback                 |

## The true comparison set

Kathai will often be compared with a specialty coffee, café dessert, premium chocolate bar, artisanal tea, evening snack or gift, not only with another hot chocolate mix. Therefore, value justification should combine servings, sensory intensity, emotional occasion and presentation.

## Pre-launch assets required

- One clear product architecture: classic signature, darker/intense option and one distinctive Indian-inspired flavor, rather than an overwhelming launch range.
- A transparent product page: ingredients, allergen information, cocoa intensity, sweetness, serving size, preparation time, storage and expected texture.
- A "choose your cup" guide based on dark, balanced or creamy preferences.
- A credible trial mechanism: paid tasting, café pop-up, office tasting, discovery sachets or small-format jar.

**Consideration principle**

Premium must be experienced as reduced uncertainty. Beautiful language attracts; precise product information closes the confidence gap.

**STAGE 3 | MAKE THE FIRST TRANSACTION FEEL SAFE AND SPECIAL**

# Purchase

For Kathai, purchase should be experience-led rather than convenience-led. At an intended price of ₹450–₹600 per drink, customers must receive more than a beverage: theatre, service, ambience, storytelling, personalization and a memorable sensory result. Digital channels should help consumers discover, reserve, pay or gift the experience, not reduce Kathai to an instantly delivered commodity. Gen Z is especially responsive to immersive, expressive and story-rich café experiences. \[13\]\[14\]\[15\]

| **Lens**     | **Customer reality**                      | **Motivation**          | **Barrier**                  | **Touchpoints**                                              | **Kathai response**                                         |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trigger**  | Mood, weather, hosting, reward or gifting | Immediate gratification | Low urgency                  | Content, café, search                                        | Use occasion-led bundles and timely availability            |
| **Format**   | First cup versus pantry experience        | Control                 | Large-experience commitment  | Guided tasting, single serve, jar                            | Create an experience ladder from first cup to repeat ritual |
| **Channel**  | Buy where convenient                      | Speed and confidence    | Channel inconsistency        | Flagship venue, pop-up, curated café partner, premium event  | Keep service, pricing, story and sensory quality coherent   |
| **Checkout** | Complete without effort                   | Ease                    | Shipping fee, COD, stock-out | Mobile checkout, UPI                                         | Clear reservation, waiting-time and payment expectations    |
| **Unboxing** | Confirm premium choice                    | Delight                 | Experience below expectation | Cupware, menu language, service cues and takeaway story card | Make the first service theatrical, personal and effortless  |

# Purchase experience ladder and measurement

| **Offer**                       | **Consumer job**                                   | **Commercial role**                | **Experience requirement**                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Signature cup / guided pop-up   | Experience the complete brand promise              | Premium category creation          | Deliver texture, aroma, temperature, storytelling and service consistently |
| Tasting flight                  | Explore cocoa intensities and stories in one visit | Encourage discovery and trade-up   | Three mini serves with guided tasting notes                                |
| Return visit / signature ritual | Turn the experience into a preferred occasion      | Core repeat revenue                | Recognisable service ritual with optional customization                    |
| Gifted experience               | Invite someone into a premium shared moment        | Acquisition through social gifting | Beautiful invitation, priority booking and take-home keepsake              |

## Purchase metrics

Track discovery-to-visit conversion, reservation completion, walk-in conversion, average spend per guest, tasting-to-signature-cup conversion, dwell time, service satisfaction, first-visit return intent and 30/60/90-day revisit behavior. Avoid optimizing for volume, discount-led traffic or low-value convenience orders.

**The first cup is the entire brand promise**

At ₹450–₹600, the customer should remember how the cup was introduced, served, tasted and shared. The product, cupware, atmosphere, interaction and story must work as one experience.

**STAGE 4 | TURN A PREMIUM TRIAL INTO A PERSONAL RITUAL**

# Retention

Trial is easier in digital commerce, but loyalty is harder because customers can compare and switch with little effort. Repeat purchase increasingly depends on product delivery, habit, trust, identity, community and relevance. \[7\] Indian direct brand relationship guidance also emphasizes owned channels, personalization and repeat behavior as growth levers. \[11\]

| **Lens**        | **Customer reality**                | **Motivation** | **Barrier**                                             | **Touchpoints**                                            | **Kathai response**                                                          |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Product**     | Taste and texture remain dependable | Trust          | Inconsistent results                                    | Service ritual, menu, staff interaction and follow-up      | Engineer repeatable service quality without making the visit feel mechanical |
| **Habit**       | Fits a recurring moment             | Comfort        | Forgotten after novelty                                 | Reminders, routine cues                                    | Create evening and weekend use cases                                         |
| Return cadence  | Easy to return before running out   | Convenience    | Limited access, long waits or inconsistent availability | WhatsApp, email, reservation access and member invitations | Invite the customer back with new stories, seasonal serves and member access |
| **Variety**     | Freshness without losing the core   | Discovery      | Flavor fatigue                                          | Seasonal drops, recipes                                    | Use limited stories around a stable signature                                |
| **Recognition** | Feels valued, not spammed           | Belonging      | Generic promotions                                      | CRM, loyalty, community                                    | Reward participation and tenure                                              |

## Retention design priorities

- Experience-led retention first: consistent taste, texture, temperature, service and atmosphere, with enough personalization to make the visit feel distinctive.
- Habit architecture: name recurring occasions and provide a visible serving ritual that is enjoyable but not cumbersome.
- Owned relationship: capture consented preferences such as intensity, milk choice, preferred occasion and gifting interest.
- Return logic: invite customers around new stories, limited menus, personal milestones and culturally relevant occasions, not arbitrary promotional calendars.
- Meaningful novelty: seasonal or regional stories should refresh interest without fragmenting operations.

**Retention hypothesis**

Kathai should aim to own one repeatable premium occasion before expanding into many occasions. The strongest launch hypothesis is an evening or weekend comfort-and-connection ritual, with Gen Z receiving additional weight through creative discovery, social identity and experience-sharing.

**WHAT MUST HAPPEN AFTER THE FIRST ORDER**

# Retention operating model

Retention should be designed as a sequence of value, not a sequence of discounts. The customer needs help achieving the intended first cup, discovering a repeatable occasion, and returning with minimal effort.

| **Moment**               | **Customer need** | **Kathai action**                                            | **Risk to avoid**                       | **Measure**                         |
| ------------------------ | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| First visit              | Confidence        | Warm hosting, guided menu choice and sensory expectation     | Too much brand copy, too little utility | Arrival-to-order conversion         |
| Within 48 hours          | Success           | A thoughtful thank-you, photo moment or story recap          | Immediate upsell                        | Experience rating and return intent |
| **Week 1-2**             | Relevance         | Suggest two occasions or recipes                             | Overcomplication                        | Second-use incidence                |
| Relevant return occasion | Convenience       | Priority reservation, new story or preferred-seat invitation | Constant discounting                    | Time to second visit                |
| **Second purchase**      | Recognition       | Thank, learn preference, unlock small benefit                | Generic points with no meaning          | Second-to-third order rate          |
| **Ongoing**              | Freshness         | Stories, seasonal recipes and community moments              | Content fatigue                         | Active repeat cohort                |

## Churn signals to investigate

- The drink is perceived as too sweet, too heavy or insufficiently distinctive for the price.
- The consumer likes it but cannot identify a frequent occasion.
- The service, venue access, wait time or total spend does not fit the intended occasion.
- A premium first impression is undermined by inconsistent service, unavailable menu items, rushed seating or unclear value.
- The brand communicates frequently but adds little practical or emotional value.

## Research questions after launch

Why did you make the first cup? When did you make it again? What did it replace? Who else consumed it? What nearly stopped you returning? Which part of the experience felt premium? These questions will sharpen persona and cohort differences more effectively than demographics alone.

**STAGE 5 | GIVE CUSTOMERS A STORY WORTH CARRYING FORWARD**

# Advocacy

Advocacy is different from satisfaction. Café research suggests that footfall and acceptable satisfaction do not automatically create recommendation; the experience must provide credibility and distinction. \[12\] For Kathai, advocacy can occur through recommendation, gifting, hosting, reviews and user-generated content.

| **Lens**           | **Customer reality**                  | **Motivation** | **Barrier**                        | **Touchpoints**                       | **Kathai response**                                   |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Social share**   | Show taste and aesthetic judgment     | Pride          | No distinctive visual cue          | Instagram, short video                | Create a recognizable pour and experienceaging moment |
| **Recommendation** | Help a friend discover a good product | Generosity     | Hard to explain differentiation    | Conversation, WhatsApp                | Provide a simple shareable proposition                |
| **Gifting**        | Express care and refinement           | Thoughtfulness | Generic hamper experience          | Festivals, corporate, occasions       | Make story and personalization integral               |
| **Review**         | Help others reduce risk               | Contribution   | Review request feels transactional | Post-purchase CRM                     | Ask about taste, use case and preparation             |
| **Community**      | Belong to a shared ritual             | Identity       | Brand community feels manufactured | Events, tastings, book/design circles | Build around meaningful occasions                     |

## Advocacy triggers

- A product result that visibly exceeds expectation: texture, aroma and balanced sweetness.
- Packaging that looks giftable without appearing wasteful or ornamental for its own sake.
- A concise story that customers can retell in one sentence.
- Recognition for hosting, reviewing, referring or contributing a personal Kathai ritual.
- Collaborations with cafés, books, design, music or regional storytellers that add cultural meaning.

**The advocacy test**

Can a customer explain Kathai without saying only "premium hot chocolate"? The desired answer is: "It is a thoughtfully crafted hot chocolate brand built around stories and comforting rituals."

**HOW THE SAME JOURNEY CHANGES BY MARKET**

# Bengaluru vs. India

| **Stage**         | **Bengaluru launch lens**                                                                                     | **India expansion lens**                                                                    | **Design implication**                                                              |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Awareness**     | Café-rich, digitally connected, experimental; premiumisation increasingly enters everyday categories. \[4\]   | Varying category familiarity; national discovery through creators, marketplaces and gifting | Use Bengaluru for concentrated seeding; scale through localized occasions and proof |
| **Consideration** | More direct comparison with specialty beverages and premium dessert brands                                    | Stronger need to clarify use, value, sweetness and preparation                              | Keep sophistication but increase functional clarity                                 |
| **Purchase**      | Flagship experiences, controlled pop-ups and a small number of curated hospitality partners can work together | Venue economics, footfall patterns and premium experience expectations differ by city       | Pilot experience economics and service repeatability before broad expansion         |
| **Retention**     | Opportunity to form office, home and monsoon rituals                                                          | Climate, language, household structure and beverage habits vary                             | Segment by occasion and cohort, not one national cadence                            |
| **Advocacy**      | Design, food and startup communities can accelerate word of mouth                                             | Gifting and regional cultural contexts broaden advocacy                                     | Create modular storytelling with a consistent master brand                          |

## Recommended sequencing

- 1\. Prove relevance in selected Bengaluru micro-markets rather than targeting the entire city.
- 2\. Validate the signature taste, preparation, price, trial format and repeat occasion.
- 3\. Build evidence through reviews, repeat cohorts, gifting behavior and organic recommendation.
- 4\. Expand to other Indian metros using the proven core while adapting occasion, language and channel mix.

**What should remain constant**

Product quality, adult premium positioning, the "story in a cup" idea, transparent information and a recognisable sensory identity. What may adapt: flavor emphasis, occasion, media mix, gifting calendar and language.

**PRE-LAUNCH DECISIONS AND POST-LAUNCH MEASURES**

# Journey scorecard

| **Stage**         | **Pre-launch decision**            | **Leading signal**                                          | **Outcome metric**                             | **Key learning question**                         |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Awareness**     | Category story and occasion        | Qualified reach, saves, search                              | Aided awareness in target cohort               | Do people understand adult premium hot chocolate? |
| **Consideration** | Claims, proof and guided tasting   | Product-page depth, tasting sign-ups                        | Guided tasting or trial conversion             | Which uncertainty blocks trial?                   |
| **Purchase**      | Experience format, price and venue | Reservation completion, walk-in conversion and availability | First-visit conversion, spend and satisfaction | Did the entire experience justify ₹450–₹600?      |
| **Retention**     | Core ritual and returnment         | Second use, return intent                                   | Second purchase and cohort repeat              | What occasion creates repeat behavior?            |
| **Advocacy**      | Share and gifting mechanics        | Reviews, referrals, UGC                                     | Recommendation and referred revenue            | What story do customers retell?                   |

## Immediate action plan

- Recruit a Bengaluru tasting panel across premium professionals, café enthusiasts, gifters and comfort-seeking home consumers.
- Test three proposition routes: craft cocoa, evening comfort ritual, and storytelling/connection.
- Test a ₹450–₹600 signature drink alongside an intensity variation; measure taste, perceived value, service expectations and willingness to revisit.
- Prototype the full journey, including discovery creative, reservation flow, arrival, menu choice, service choreography, cupware, story moment, payment, farewell and return invitation.
- Update this map after the first 100 tastings, first 100 paid orders and first meaningful repeat-purchase cohort.

**Decision rule**

Do not scale awareness until Kathai can demonstrate value clarity, a consistently memorable ₹450–₹600 experience and an identifiable repeat occasion.
